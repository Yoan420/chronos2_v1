# NYX: implementation and information-set audit

Audit date: 16 September 2026. Read-only inspection; no model was trained, no historical result was regenerated, and no existing artifact was changed for this audit.

All repository paths below are relative to `<workspace>`. Line numbers refer to the inspected source. Define `R(z) = runs/experiments/nuclear_forecast_v1/2026-09-16/{z}/civil_pit_v2`, where `z` is `be`, `de`, `fr`, or `nl`. The evidential priority is the frozen result and resolved configuration, followed by the code that generated them; prose descriptions alone are not evidence that an option was executed. The preliminary description in `tmp/tensor_timesfm_audit/nyx_architecture.md` was checked against these sources.

## 1. Evaluated system and scope

The evaluated NYX system is `nuclear_kalman`: a fixed Chronos-2 checkpoint, a daily rolling CatBoost residual correction, and a governed daily rolling Kalman correction. Internally, these stages are `chronos2`, `residual_corrected`, and `residual_kalman`. The intermediate `nuclear_autonomous` output is the Chronos-plus-CatBoost ablation. French nuclear-generation forecasts enter all three stages; for Germany, Belgium, and the Netherlands they remain **French** nuclear forecasts. Neither Storm nor MKonline is a model input. There is no LoRA adaptation and no Chronos fine-tuning in this pipeline. Sources: `NUCLEAR_FORECAST.md:9–20`; `chronos2_hourly/nuclear_forecast.py:631–675`; frozen `R(z)/report_only/frozen_result/audits.json`, `result` object.

The independently documented `nuclear_kalman_extreme_v1` experiment is **not** part of these bundles. Its additional boosted-tree expert, 90-day startup, weekly refits, EUR 10 activation threshold, EUR 100 raw cap, and weights 0/0.25/0.5 must not be described as NYX components evaluated here. Sources: `NUCLEAR_KALMAN_EXTREME.md:1–43`; the engine and recipe recorded in the four frozen bundles.

Each frozen result evaluates 365 local delivery dates, **16 September 2025 through 15 September 2026**, with 8,760 hourly observations per country. The four-country total before any comparator-specific availability mask is 35,040 country-hours. The 16 September 2026 delivery is an additional forecast, not part of this realized annual evaluation. The engine keeps 730 days of upstream forecast history, with an anchored diagnostic startup; the final annual evaluation follows this warmup. The frozen audit explicitly calls the warmup `fixed_epoch_diagnostic_warmup_not_full_nested_validation`. Sources: `chronos2_hourly/nuclear_forecast.py:647–663`; `R(z)/report_only/frozen_result/audits.json`, `result` and `kalman_replay` objects.

The rolling windows contain 365 **civil days**, not a constant 8,760-row slice: the recorded Kalman training-window sizes range from 8,759 to 8,761 hours because of daylight-saving transitions. Days of 23 and 25 hours are retained. Forecast horizons therefore adapt to the physical delivery day despite the nominal configuration value of 24. Sources: `R(z)/resolved_config.yaml:19–32,42–45`; `chronos2_hourly/nuclear_forecast.py:304–308`; `chronos2_hourly/kalman_residual.py:1439–1471`; frozen `kalman_replay` audit.

## 2. Data, timestamps, and what the cutoff proves

The target is the hourly day-ahead price in EUR/MWh for each bidding zone. Six forecast fundamentals are used: residual-load forecasts for FR, DE, BE, NL, and ES, plus French nuclear-generation forecasts, all in GW. Their aliases and frozen files appear in `R(fr)/resolved_config.yaml:34–40`. The nuclear series is `power.fr.generation.nuclear.gw.fcst`; residual-load series follow `power.{country}.residual.load.hourly.gw.fcst`. Calendar covariates are deterministic.

For delivery day D, the covariate cutoff is **08:00 local civil time on D−1**, bounded above by the declared `runtime_as_of`. The preparation layer computes the previous civil date before constructing 08:00, preventing a one-hour DST drift. It admits rows whose declared snapshot and revision timestamps are both no later than the cutoff and selects the latest admissible record. It does not replace a latest non-finite value by an older finite value. Complete future coverage is required, interpolation and filling are disabled, and realized nuclear generation is not used as a substitute. Sources: `chronos2_hourly/nuclear_preparation.py:51–58,99–129`; `R(fr)/resolved_config.yaml:20–32`; `chronos2_hourly/nuclear_forecast.py:200–211,310–316`.

This is a **reconstructed as-of protocol**, not independently certified provider-publication provenance. Historical materialization queries the provider with `revision_date=cutoff_utc`, then writes that same query cutoff into both `snapshot_time_utc` and `revision_time_utc`; actual extraction time is stored separately. The provider's original publication timestamp is not independently returned. All four preparation audits report `provider_revision_timestamp_available=false` and `production_pit_evidence=false`. Sources: `materialize_saturn_daily_asof.py:309–318,347–353`; `chronos2_hourly/nuclear_sources.py:35–38,148–172`; `R(z)/prepared/pit_selection_audit.json:12–13`. Consequently, filtering these metadata proves compliance with the implemented selection rule, but cannot establish that all values exactly reproduce the information a trader actually received at each historical origin.

The price context needs a separate explanation. It ends at **23:00 local time on D−1**, rather than at 08:00. Backtest origins are array positions of the first delivery hour, and Chronos receives all preceding target hours. Sources: `chronos2_hourly/chronos_adapter.py:605–609,636–658`; `chronos2_modular/forecasting.py:239–247`; the live contract in `chronos2_hourly/chronos_adapter.py:706–720`. For the frozen delivery, `runtime_as_of_utc` is 15 September 2026 06:00 UTC while the last target delivery timestamp is 21:00 UTC that day (`R(z)/prepared/pit_selection_audit.json:5,22`). This difference alone does **not** establish leakage: day-ahead prices can be published before their delivery hour. However, the target snapshot has no distinct publication field proving that availability for every historical context observation. Historical targets also use the latest values returned by Saturn at extraction, not separately preserved price vintages. Sources: `run_multicountry_forecast.py:1306–1341`; recent-target merging in `run_nuclear_forecast.py:543–558`. The article must distinguish delivery time, assumed market availability, and evidenced publication time.

### Hourly representation across the SDAC resolution change

Every frozen configuration specifies `target_input_resolution: hourly`. In this mode the loader validates an hourly index; the separate `quarter_hour` path averages four complete quarter-hour values, but it is not the configured path here. Sources: `R(z)/resolved_config.yaml:20`; `chronos2_hourly/hourly_contract.py:142–167,221–263`. Observed prices are fetched from the explicitly hourly series `power.price.da.<zone>.bzn.hourly.entsoe.utc.cdh.eurmwh`, without a local four-quarter aggregation in that reader (`run_multicountry_forecast.py:1306–1325`).

Thus the evaluated response is the **hourly representation supplied by the data source**. Even if the article independently documents the SDAC transition on 1 October 2025, this repository inspection does not verify that every subsequent hourly source value equals the arithmetic mean of the four native market prices. It does not support claims about native quarter-hour prediction accuracy. A source-level definition or reconciliation against native quarter-hours would be needed to call the response a verified four-quarter average.

Storm is also fetched from hourly sources. The reader requests an hourly `.da.cache` series without `revision_date`, overlays it on a latest native hourly fallback, and does not resample native quarter-hours (`chronos2_hourly/storm_dashboard.py:104–158`). It therefore is not an audited comparator reconstructed at the same 08:00 origin. Autumn singleton hours are assigned to the standard-time fold rather than duplicated (`storm_dashboard.py:771–777,821–855`). All paired metrics must disclose the common finite mask and effective sample count. The FR reporting source audit, for example, records 8,783/8,784 Storm hours including 504 fallback hours; this is a reporting-source coverage count, not the final 365-day evaluation denominator (`R(fr)/report_only/sources/20260915T120651322085Z_65a44cc6/statistics_history_audit.json:246–249,288–292`).

## 3. Frozen Chronos-2 backbone

All four configurations specify `amazon/chronos-2`, checkpoint revision **`29ec3766d36d6f73f0696f85560a422f50e8498c`**, local-files-only loading, seed 42, context length **2,048 hours**, nominal horizon 24, origin batch size 12, and model batch size 128. The adapter groups physical days by their actual 23/24/25-hour horizons. Sources: `R(z)/resolved_config.yaml:1–11,23,45`; `chronos2_hourly/chronos_adapter.py:626–658`.

Historical price context, historical forecast-fundamental channels, and deterministic calendar channels are passed jointly. The future contains the six known forecast channels and calendar channels. The repository's future strategy name `oracle` merely passes through a covariate declared known-future; here those inputs are the selected forecast vintages, not realized future fundamentals. Sources: `chronos2_modular/data.py:1465–1511`; `chronos2_modular/forecasting.py:239–275`. Calendar includes periodic hour, weekday, and day-of-year terms (`chronos2_modular/data.py:1150–1185`); excluding day-of-year from the later CatBoost feature builder does not remove it from Chronos.

The code calls `Chronos2Pipeline.from_pretrained` with the pinned revision, then `predict_df` with `cross_learning=False`, `validate_inputs=False`, the specified context length, and a batch size of at least the number of variates. It requests nine deciles, 0.1 through 0.9; the downstream hourly pipeline retains q10/q50/q90. Sources: `chronos2_modular/forecasting.py:27–64,397–410`; `chronos2_modular/common.py:20`; quantile normalization in `chronos2_hourly/chronos_adapter.py:326–408`. The median is the point forecast used by the residual models. The call is covariate-informed inference with fixed foundation-model weights, not country-specific gradient training.

`device: auto` is a configuration choice, not evidence of the actual execution hardware. The resolver selects CUDA if available, otherwise CPU; bfloat16 is conditional on CUDA support, otherwise float32 (`chronos2_modular/common.py:380–395`). No audited hardware/precision log was found in the inspected bundle. Do not infer GPU execution, a specific precision, or inference latency from this setting.

Chronos-2's primary paper was first submitted **17 October 2025**, after the evaluation begins ([Ansari et al., Chronos-2, arXiv:2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15821)). The pinned local revision is resolved from currently available files (`run_nuclear_forecast.py:505–512`). A checkpoint pin establishes identity; it does not prove historical deployability before release or exclude overlap between evaluation data and foundation-model pretraining. Accordingly, describe this as a retrospective application of a fixed checkpoint. Do not claim a year of actual historical service, a pre-release operational simulation, or independently verified pretraining-disjoint zero-shot evaluation.

## 4. Daily CatBoost residual adaptation

For each country and delivery day D, let c(D,h,q) be the Chronos quantile and y(D,h) the observed price. The residual regressor is fit on rows whose local delivery dates satisfy **D−365 ≤ d < D**, using target r(d,h) = y(d,h) − c(d,h,0.5). It is refit every day. At least 720 training rows are required; before this minimum the output is the uncorrected Chronos forecast and the cold start is recorded. Source: `chronos2_hourly/nuclear_forecast.py:272–387`, especially `339,348–358`; `chronos2_hourly/models/residual_corrector.py:1115–1145`.

The effective CatBoost recipe is MAE loss and evaluation metric, 700 iterations, depth 6, learning rate 0.03, L2 leaf regularization 15, seed 42, four threads in the recorded run, `has_time=True`, `nan_mode="Min"`, and no model-file writing. The supplied `min_samples_leaf: 30` field is **not passed to CatBoostRegressor** and must not be represented as an effective CatBoost constraint. There is no validation `eval_set` or early stopping in this fitting call. Sources: `R(fr)/resolved_config.yaml:80–105`; the recorded effective `result.residual_recipe` in frozen audits; `chronos2_hourly/models/residual_corrector.py:1029–1046,1134–1145`. The CLI overrides the general thread value (`chronos2_hourly/nuclear_forecast.py:475–477`).

The feature builder includes selected forecast fundamentals, Chronos quantiles and interval width, deterministic calendar and multi-country holidays, interactions, missingness indicators, and whole-delivery-day profiles of known forecasts. Profiles include mean, standard deviation, range, centered values, and ramps; they use forecast channels available under the reconstructed origin protocol, not realized delivery-day prices. Historical-price-derived columns and day-of-year columns are excluded from this residual stage. Sources: `chronos2_hourly/models/residual_corrector.py:351–422,447–533,730–857`. The full fitted feature schema is recorded in `residual_daily_audit.parquet`. The generic LEAR, alternative CatBoost, ensemble, and five-fold OOF sections elsewhere in the YAML are **not** the recipe invoked by this nuclear residual replay; the active factory is `run_chronos2_hourly.py:486–517`.

Writing f_D(X(D,h)) for the fitted residual prediction, the applied shift is

    delta(D,h) = clip(f_D(X(D,h)), −40, +40),
    b(D,h,q) = c(D,h,q) + delta(D,h).

The correction scale is 1.0 and the same EUR/MWh shift is added to each retained quantile (`chronos2_hourly/models/residual_corrector.py:1177–1204`). CatBoost adapts to recent errors, but no gradient update reaches Chronos. Historical replay rows are checked for finite labels, complete contiguous civil days, correct origins, and exclusion of D's labels (`chronos2_hourly/nuclear_forecast.py:290–327`). These are programmatic checks under the declared information model, not a substitute for source-vintage certification.

## 5. Governed rolling Kalman overlay

For each final evaluation day D, all candidate states are initialized afresh and replayed on the complete preceding 365 civil days. State prediction occurs once per day; each training day's candidate forecast is computed before its labels are assimilated, then hourly observations update the state. D's entire forecast is generated without assimilating any D outcome. The algorithm is therefore a daily rolling refit, not one continuously carried filter spanning the entire experiment. Sources: `chronos2_hourly/kalman_residual.py:1439–1624`.

Five candidates are enabled; the audit's generic algorithm name containing `ekf` does not make an EKF candidate active:

| Candidate | State / proposed additive correction to b |
|---|---|
| `linear_bias` | One bias; g = bias. |
| `linear_harmonic` | Three coefficients; g = bias + a sin(hour) + b cos(hour). |
| `linear_market` | Fourteen coefficients: intercept, hour sine/cosine, standardized upstream median, interval width, CatBoost shift, five residual-load forecasts, their mean and range, and French nuclear forecast. |
| `linear_scale` | Bias and scale s; g = bias + (s−1) delta, with s clipped to [0.5,1.5]. |
| `ukf_scale` | Bias and scale logit u; g = bias + (s(u)−1) delta, with s(u) = 0.5 + sigmoid(u). |

Sources: `chronos2_hourly/kalman_residual.py:125–130,877–988,999–1010`; `chronos2_hourly/nuclear_forecast.py:200–211`. The residual-load “spread” is the maximum minus the minimum, not a standard deviation (`chronos2_hourly/kalman_covariates.py:428–432`). The market candidate's continuous inputs are centered by training-window medians and scaled by max(1.4826 MAD, population standard deviation, 10⁻⁶), then clipped to [−5,5]. Observation variance is R = max(s²,1), with s obtained from the same robust-scale expression for upstream errors y−b. Sources: `chronos2_hourly/kalman_residual.py:793–825,861–869`.

For linear candidates, the daily prior is m⁻ = A m and P⁻ = A P Aᵀ + Q. A is identity; for the UKF scale logit the daily persistence is 0.99. Initial means are zero except linear scale, initialized at 1. Initial covariance is R I, except the scale coordinate, initialized at 0.05². Daily process variance is 0.001 R per ordinary coordinate; the scale coordinate uses 0.0001 × 0.05². Sources: `chronos2_hourly/kalman_residual.py:111–124,896–958,992–997`.

An hourly linear update with regressor x uses K = P x / (xᵀ P x + R), m ← m + K nu, and P ← P − K xᵀ P, with covariance safeguards. Here nu is the raw innovation clipped to **±3 sqrt(R)**; it is not clipped by the full predictive innovation standard deviation. Bias, harmonic and market candidates observe y−b; the scale candidates observe y−c with measurement bias + s delta. The nonlinear scale variant uses the additive unscented filter. Sources: `chronos2_hourly/kalman_residual.py:1032–1048,1132–1256`. There is no smoothing and no EM parameter estimation in the recorded replay.

The raw hourly correction from every candidate is clipped to ±20 EUR/MWh. A governor examines the latest 60 training days, requires at least 14, and minimizes pooled hourly MAE over candidate weights {0,0.05,…,1}. It accepts the best candidate only when improvement over the CatBoost upstream is at least max(0.05 EUR/MWh, 0.005 × baseline MAE); otherwise it selects identity with weight zero. One candidate and weight are used for the entire target day. The configured confirmation window is zero, so there is no independent confirmation subset. Sources: `chronos2_hourly/kalman_residual.py:108–130,1287–1396,1583–1624`.

The final forecast is

    NYX(D,h,q) = b(D,h,q) + w_D clip(g_D,h, −20, +20).

The additive deviation from Chronos is consequently bounded by 60 EUR/MWh. Both post-processors translate all three quantiles equally: interval widths and ordering are preserved, but calibrated coverage, tail correction, joint scenario coherence, or annual non-degradation do not follow from this construction.

### Important qualification of the governor's internal replay

The outer D forecast excludes D labels. However, market normalization and R are estimated using **all 365 training days before replaying those days** (`chronos2_hourly/kalman_residual.py:1502–1513`). Consequently, the saved candidate predictions on the trailing 60 days are prequential with respect to state assimilation but are not independently nested, original-origin forecasts: their scaling and variance can depend on later observations within the training window. In addition, the same 60 days select and score the candidate/weight. This does not introduce a target-D label into the outer prediction, but it makes the governor's retrospective improvement an internal selection diagnostic, not an unbiased independent validation estimate. The article should not label it fully nested OOF validation.

All four frozen audits report zero target-day assimilation, zero future-observation assimilation, no smoother, and no EM. These fields support the intended execution contract; they do not prove provider-vintage integrity or exclude all unrecorded research-selection effects. The paper should disclose whether model design, caps, and reporting choices were settled after examining this year; the inspected files do not establish preregistration.

All four final run results record `forecast_result_reused: true`. Their final report-generation start/end timestamps therefore are not the original Chronos-plus-daily-refits computation time. No latency or computational-cost result should be inferred from those durations. Sources: `R(z)/run_result.json`, execution object; `R(z)/run_status.json`.

A faithful reproduction requires the frozen target and forecast-vintage snapshots, their source/query semantics, the pinned Chronos checkpoint, the exact active residual/Kalman recipe, local civil-day mapping, and the same rolling replay. Model inference needs no network once the checkpoint is local. The underlying Saturn extracts are organization-specific; absent permission to redistribute them, the paper should state that data access is restricted and provide schemas, hashes, parameters, and evaluation code rather than imply public end-to-end reproducibility.

## 7. Suggested manuscript wording

“We evaluate NYX retrospectively on hourly day-ahead price series for Belgium, Germany, France, and the Netherlands from 16 September 2025 to 15 September 2026. NYX combines a fixed Chronos-2 checkpoint using 2,048 hours of context and six forecast fundamentals with a daily CatBoost residual model trained on the previous 365 civil days and a governed rolling Kalman overlay. CatBoost and Kalman shifts are bounded by 40 and 20 EUR/MWh, respectively, and are applied equally to the retained 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9 quantiles. No foundation-model fine-tuning or LoRA is performed.

“Forecast covariates are reconstructed through historical as-of queries at 08:00 local time on D−1. Their stored revision timestamps are query cutoffs rather than independent provider-publication evidence. Historical price revisions, comparator vintages and foundation-model pretraining overlap are not fully certified. The checkpoint postdates the beginning of the test period; results therefore characterize a retrospective application under the reconstructed information protocol, not a verified historical production deployment. Internal Kalman governance uses prequential state updates within each training window, but normalization and variance are estimated on that entire window and its trailing selection segment is not an independent nested validation set. Results concern the hourly series supplied by the sources and do not establish native quarter-hour forecasting performance.”
