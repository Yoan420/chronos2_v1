ABSTRACT 
In recent years, extreme electricity price events have become more frequent and severe due 
to increased renewable energy integration and external market shocks, posing significant 
risks to market participants and system operators. Accurate forecasting of such events is 
essential for supporting market stability, operational planning, and risk management. This 
paper proposes an adaptive dynamic weighted threshold (DWT) approach to identify 
extreme price events under dynamically changing market conditions. To address the chal -
lenge of imbalanced data, a weighted-XGBoost (W-XGB) classification model is developed to 
forecast extreme price occurrences. Comparative experiments demonstrate that the pro -
posed method outperforms baseline models in predictive performance and class discrimin -
ation. To enhance interpretability, SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is applied to 
quantify feature contributions and provide insights into the drivers of extreme price events. 
Results show that extremely high prices are influenced by multiple factors, including supply- 
demand dynamics, fuel price volatility, and historical patterns, while extremely low prices are 
primarily driven by residual load, reflecting a more deterministic structure. The proposed 
framework integrates adaptive thresholding, imbalanced learning, and interpretable analysis, 
providing a practical tool to support decision-making in electricity markets. It offers action -
able insights to inform bidding strategies, operational decisions, and risk management in 
increasingly volatile electricity markets.
PRACTITIONER SUMMARY 
Extreme electricity price events have become more frequent due to increased renewable 
energy integration and external shocks, posing significant risks to electricity market partici -
pants and system operators. These events directly impact bidding strategies, operational 
planning, and risk management decisions. This study introduces a practical and interpretable 
machine learning framework to improve the forecasting of extreme electricity price occur -
rences. A dynamic weighted threshold (DWT) method is proposed to identify extreme prices 
more accurately by adapting to changing market conditions. To forecast these events, a 
weighted-XGBoost model is employed, which effectively handles the rarity of extreme price 
events in historical data. SHAP-based explainability is applied to identify the key drivers of 
extreme prices. The results show that extremely high prices are influenced by multiple inter -
acting factors, while extremely low prices are more closely related to renewable generation 
and residual load conditions. The proposed framework provides actionable insights to sup -
port practical decision-making in volatile electricity markets.

1. Introduction
With the deregulation of European electricity mar -
kets in the late twentieth century, market partici -
pants and system operators have become 
increasingly exposed to significant price risks. 
Unlike traditional commodities, electricity cannot be 
efficiently stored, and its transmission is constrained 
by the physical limitations of the grid (Souhir et al., 
2019 ). Consequently, electricity spot prices exhibit 
higher volatility and are characterised by extreme 
behaviours, including sharp spikes and even 
negative prices. These extreme price dynamics pose 
substantial challenges for operational planning, risk 
management, and market decision-making, as par -
ticipants and operators need to continuously adapt 
their decisions under highly uncertain price 
conditions.
In recent years, the increasing penetration of 
renewable energy and the occurrence of external 
shocks have further intensified electricity price vola -
tility. Renewable energy sources such as wind and 
photovoltaic power are inherently intermittent,

during price spikes. In addition, system operators 
rely on accurate forecasts to manage supply–demand 
imbalances, ensure system reliability, and monitor 
potential market power abuse (Westgaard et al., 
2021 ). Therefore, robust identification and forecasting 
of extreme price events are crucial for supporting 
operational planning, risk management, and regula -
tory oversight in electricity markets.
1.1. Definition and occurrences of extreme price
The occurrence of extreme electricity prices can be 
formulated as a binary classification problem, where 
price observations are categorised into normal and 
extreme events based on predefined thresholds 
(Hagfors et al., 2016 ). Since extreme prices are not 
directly observable, determining an appropriate 
threshold is a critical first step in identifying such 
events.
From an operational perspective, threshold defi -
nitions play a key role in decision-making. In prac -
tice, extreme price signals are used to trigger risk 
management actions, such as adjusting bidding 
strategies, activating hedging mechanisms, or imple -
menting system interventions. Therefore, the way in 
which extreme prices are defined directly affects 
downstream operational and trading decisions.
In the literature, two main approaches have been 
used to define extreme price thresholds: fixed 
thresholds and variable thresholds. Fixed thresholds 
specify a constant price level (e.g., 100 or 150 EUR/ 
MWh) (Clements et al., 2015 ; Galarneau-Vincent 
et al., 2023 ; He & Chen, 2016 ; Herrera & Gonz �alez, 
2014 ; Manner et al., 2016 ), while variable thresholds 
are typically defined using statistical quantiles, such 
as the 90th or 95th percentile for high prices and 
the 10th or 5th percentile for low prices (Liu et al., 
2022a ; Sandhu et al., 2016 ; Stefan et al., 2007 ). 
Compared to fixed thresholds, variable thresholds 
offer greater flexibility by adapting to the distribu -
tion of electricity prices.
In practice, quantile-based thresholds are more 
widely adopted due to their adaptability, and thus 
form the focus of this study. However, despite this 
advantage, they typically rely on static quantiles 
computed over the entire dataset. This global, time- 
invariant perspective tends to overemphasise periods 
affected by external shocks, such as geopolitical con -
flicts, extreme weather, or policy changes, which 
cause sharp price fluctuations while other periods 
remain relatively stable. As a result, extreme price 
classification becomes biased towards these volatile 
years, potentially overlooking high prices during 
more typical market conditions.
More importantly, current approaches lack the 
ability to balance stability and adaptability when 
defining extreme price events. From a decision-mak -
ing perspective, unstable or poorly calibrated thresh -
olds may lead to either delayed responses to 
extreme events or overreactions to short-term fluc -
tuations, both of which can result in suboptimal 
operational and trading decisions. This limitation 
highlights the need for a more adaptive and flexible 
approach to accurately identify extreme electricity 
price occurrences.
1.2. Methods for forecasting extreme price 
occurrences
A wide range of approaches has been proposed to 
forecast the occurrence of extreme electricity prices.

Traditional statistical methods, such as autoregres -
sive models and logistic regression, have been widely 
used due to their interpretability and well-estab -
lished theoretical foundations (Adline & Ikeda, 
2023 ; Eichler et al., 2014 ; Liu et al., 2022a ; Manner 
et al., 2016 ; Maryniak & Weron, 2019 ). However, 
these approaches often struggle to capture the non -
linear relationships and complex interactions among 
variables inherent in electricity markets, particularly 
under highly volatile conditions.
In recent years, machine learning (ML) methods 
have demonstrated superior performance in model -
ling nonlinear dependencies and handling high- 
dimensional data (Galarneau-Vincent et al., 2023 ). 
As a result, various ML models, including random 
forests (RF) (Datta & Datta, 2016 ; Galarneau- 
Vincent et al., 2023 ; He & Chen, 2016 ), deep neural 
networks (DNNs) (Yamada & Mori, 2021 ; Liu et al., 
2022b ), and gradient boosting decision trees 
(GBDT) (Galarneau-Vincent et al., 2023 ; Stathakis 
et al., 2021 ; Zamudio L �opez et al., 2024 ), have been 
increasingly applied to electricity price forecasting.
Despite these advances, forecasting extreme price 
occurrences remains particularly challenging. 
Extreme events are rare and often exhibit abrupt 
and irregular patterns, making them difficult to cap -
ture even for advanced machine learning models. 
Moreover, most existing studies primarily focus on 
improving predictive accuracy, while paying limited 
attention to model interpretability and how the 
resulting insights can support decision-making in 
practice.
Among ML approaches, boosting-based ensemble 
methods, particularly Extreme Gradient Boosting 
(XGBoost), have emerged as state-of-the-art techni -
ques due to their strong predictive performance and 
computational efficiency (Coenen et al., 2022 ; 
Rawson et al., 2022 ; Yao et al., 2024 ). XGBoost is 
especially effective in capturing nonlinear relation -
ships and complex feature interactions, which are 
characteristic of electricity price dynamics. However, 
existing applications of XGBoost mainly focus on 
forecasting general electricity prices, while its appli -
cation to forecasting extreme price occurrences, par -
ticularly in an interpretable and decision-oriented 
framework, remains limited.
1.3. Challenges on classification of imbalanced 
data and its interpretability
In practice, occurrences of extremely high or low 
prices are relatively rare compared to normal price 
events, resulting in a highly imbalanced dataset. 
This imbalance poses a significant challenge for 
standard machine learning algorithms, which tend 
to be biased towards the majority class and may fail 
to accurately identify rare but critical extreme price 
events (Averro et al., 2023 ; Ruisen et al., 2018 ). As a 
result, effectively handling class imbalance is essen -
tial for reliable forecasting of extreme electricity 
price occurrences.
Existing approaches to address class imbalance 
can be broadly categorised into data-level and algo -
rithm-level methods (Tanha et al., 2020 ). Data-level 
approaches aim to rebalance the dataset through 
resampling techniques such as over-sampling, 
under-sampling, and SMOTE (Del Rio et al., 2015 ; 
Dhankhad et al., 2018 ; Varmedja et al., 2019 ). While 
these methods can improve class balance, they may 
alter the original data distribution and introduce 
additional noise, which can be problematic in time- 
series settings where preserving temporal dependen -
cies and chronological structure is essential.
Algorithm-level approaches, on the other hand, 
modify the learning process to account for class 
imbalance (Tanha et al., 2020 ; Wang et al., 2020 ). In 
particular, class-weighting mechanisms adjust the 
importance of minority class observations within the 
model training process, enabling more balanced 
classification performance without modifying the 
input data. This is especially advantageous for time- 
series forecasting problems, where maintaining the 
chronological structure of the data is essential for 
avoiding information distortion and ensuring realis -
tic modelling.
Given the characteristics of extreme electricity 
price events, weighted-XGBoost provides a suitable 
modelling framework by combining the advantages 
of boosting algorithms with an effective class- 
weighting mechanism (Averro et al., 2023 ). It is cap -
able of capturing complex nonlinear relationships 
while simultaneously addressing class imbalance in a 
principled and efficient manner.
In addition to predictive performance, interpret -
ability plays a crucial role in the practical applica -
tion of forecasting models. Market participants and 
system operators are not only interested in accurate 
forecasts but also in understanding the key drivers 
of extreme price events. Such insights are essential 
for supporting risk management, improving bidding 
strategies, and enhancing operational decision-mak -
ing in electricity markets.
Explainable Artificial Intelligence (xAI) has 
emerged as an important area of research to address 
the interpretability of complex machine learning 
models (Burkart & Huber, 2021 ; Molnar, 2020 ). 
Among various methods, SHAP (SHapley Additive 
exPlanations) provides a unified framework for 
quantifying the contribution of each input feature to 
model predictions (Lundberg & Lee, 2017 ).
Although SHAP has been widely applied in elec -
tricity system studies, existing work has primarily

1.4. Research aims and contributions
To address the gaps, this study develops an inter -
pretable machine learning framework for forecasting 
imbalanced extreme electricity price occurrences 
and analysing the contribution of different input 
features. The proposed framework integrates a 
dynamic weighted threshold method for identifying 
extreme price events with a weighted-XGBoost 
model for forecasting their occurrence. It bench -
marks forecasting performance against Logistic 
Regression and Random Forest models with class 
weighting, and incorporates multiple sources of 
information, including historical electricity prices, 
market characteristics (e.g., load and generation), 
fuel prices, and a geopolitical risk indicator. 
Furthermore, SHapley Additive exPlanations 
(SHAP) are employed to quantify feature contribu -
tions and uncover the key drivers of extreme price 
occurrences. This enables the proposed framework 
to provide not only accurate forecasts but also inter -
pretable insights that directly support risk manage -
ment, operational planning, and decision-making in 
electricity markets.
Overall, the proposed framework bridges fore -
casting modelling and decision support by integrat -
ing adaptive threshold design, imbalanced learning, 
and interpretable analysis for extreme electricity 
price forecasting. The key contributions of this 
paper are summarised as follows.
� Proposing a dynamic weighted threshold method 
for identifying extreme electricity prices, which 
balances stability and adaptability under varying 
market conditions. Compared to conventional 
static or rolling quantile-based thresholds, the 
proposed approach enables more robust and 
context-sensitive identification of extreme price 
events.
� Incorporating the Geopolitical Risk Index as a 
novel feature in extreme electricity price forecast -
ing, examining its potential impact in the context 
of geopolitical dynamics.
� Developing a weighted-XGBoost-based forecast -
ing model that effectively addresses class imbal -
ance while preserving the temporal structure of 
the data. This improves the model’s ability to 
forecast rare extreme price events without dis -
torting the original time-series characteristics.
� Applying SHAP-based explainability to decom -
pose model results and assess feature contribu -
tion. This enables stakeholders to better 
understand the underlying mechanisms of 
extreme events and supports more informed risk 
management and operational decision-making.

Section 3 reports results and discussion, including

2.1. Data
This research focuses primarily on the European 
electricity spot market. Five years of data from 
January 1, 2019, to December 31, 2023, are chosen 
as this period primarily encompasses the European 
energy crisis. In addition, since Germany’s day- 
ahead electricity market is one of the most actively 
traded spot markets with a high penetration of 
renewable energy resources in Europe (Lehna et al., 
2022 ), German data is selected for research. For 
power market data, day-ahead (DA) electricity pri -
ces, residual load forecasts, total load forecasts, and 
total generation forecasts in the German market are 
obtained from the European Network of 
Transmission System Operators for Electricity 
(ENTSOE) platform (Entsoe, 2026 ), as well as 
renewable energy-related input variables: wind and 
photovoltaics generation forecasts.
Although renewable energy resources already 
account for more than half of Germany’s electricity 
production, coal- and natural gas-based power gen -
eration continues to be a crucial complement to 
intermittent generation (Madadkhani & Ikonnikova, 
2024 ). Therefore, EGIX (European Gas Index) and 
API2 (ARA coal price) indexes are considered in

2.3. Identifying extreme prices
With the input features established, the next step is 
to define the target variable for forecasting extreme 
electricity prices. This study formulates the task as a 
supervised binary classification problem. Traditional 
approaches often rely on a static percentile com -
puted over the entire dataset, which fails to effect -
ively adapt to temporal variations in the market. To 
enhance extreme price identification, we propose an 
adaptive, dynamic weighted threshold (DWT) 
method, integrating static global quantiles (capturing 
overall market trends) with rolling local quantiles 
(reflecting short-term fluctuations). The weights are 
dynamically adjusted based on market volatility, 
allowing the thresholds to adapt effectively to price 
variations.
This approach, inspired by applications of rolling 
quantile in risk management, volatility forecasting, 
and trading strategy optimisation (Jiang et al., 2019 ; 
Marshall et al., 2017 ; Packham et al., 2017 ), ensures 
more responsive classification compared to static 
quantile methods.

2.4.4. Hyperparameter optimisation
Furthermore, some parameters cannot be deter -
mined through the training process above and must 
be specified in advance. Machine learning com -
monly uses hyperparameter optimisation techniques 
to set these parameters in order to achieve the best 
forecasting performance. Common approaches 
include grid search, random search, and Bayesian 
optimisation (Bischl et al., 2023 ; Srinivas & Katarya, 
2022 ; Yang & Shami, 2020 ). Grid and random 
search exhaustively explore the entire parameter 
space, while Bayesian optimisation uses prior infor -
mation to guide a more efficient search process. By 
leveraging probabilistic modelling, Bayesian opti -
misation can identify optimal hyperparameters with 
fewer evaluations (Lindauer et al., 2022 ; Stuke et al., 
2021 ; Turner et al., 2021 ). Therefore, this study 
adopts Bayesian optimisation for hyperparameter 
tuning. Several key hyperparameters are selected for 
optimising weighted-XGBoost in this study, includ -
ing the following:
1. max_depth: Defines the maximum depth of 
each tree, controlling the model’s complexity.
2. learning rate ðgÞ: Determines the step size dur -
ing optimisation, affecting model convergence.
3. n_estimators: Specifies the number of boosting 
rounds, balancing model accuracy and compu -
tational cost.
4. gamma ðcÞ: A regularisation term that controls 
tree complexity by setting a minimum loss 
reduction required for a split.
5. lambda ðkÞ: Applies an l
2 
penalty on leaf weights 
to prevent overfitting and improve generalisation.
6. scale_pos_weight: Adjusts the penalty for the 
minority class, enhancing model performance 
on imbalanced datasets.

The SHAP analysis presented in the previous section

tive and relate them to underlying market mecha -
nisms and existing literature.

A key insight from the SHAP results is the asym -
metry between extremely high and low price events. 
While prior studies have also documented differen -
ces between price spikes and negative prices 
(Hagfors et al., 2016 ), our results suggest that this 
asymmetry has become more pronounced under 
recent market conditions. Extremely high prices are 
driven by the combined influence of multiple fac -
tors, whereas extremely low prices are largely domi -
nated by a single key variable, namely residual load. 
This reflects distinct economic mechanisms underly -
ing price spikes and price collapses.
Residual load emerges as one of the most influen -
tial variables. As a measure of net demand after 
accounting for renewable generation, higher residual 
load indicates tighter supply–demand conditions, 
requiring the dispatch of higher-cost generation 
units. This increases marginal prices and the likeli -
hood of price spikes. Conversely, low residual load, 
which is often associated with high renewable gener -
ation, can lead to excess supply and even negative 
prices. This explains the more concentrated and 
deterministic pattern observed for extremely low 
prices.
Fuel-related variables, such as gas and coal prices, 
also play a significant role. Increases in fuel prices 
raise the marginal cost of thermal generation, shift -
ing the supply curve upward and leading to higher 
market-clearing prices. This is consistent with the 
merit-order framework widely used in the electricity 
economics literature (Do et al., 2019 ; Nowotarski & 
Weron, 2018 ), and helps explain the multi-factor 
nature of extremely high price events, particularly 
under conditions of fuel price volatility.
Lagged electricity prices further contribute by 
capturing temporal dependence and persistence in 
market conditions. Price spikes tend to cluster over 
time, reflecting sustained supply constraints or pro -
longed demand surges, which adds to the complex -
ity of extreme high price dynamics.
In addition, the Geopolitical Risk Index was 
included to capture broader economic and political 
disruptions. While prior studies suggest that geopol -
itical risk significantly influences electricity prices in 
general electricity price dynamics (Abdullah et al., 
2023 ; Sa ^adaoui & Jabeur, 2023 ), its relatively lower 
importance in our analysis reflects its infrequent 
occurrence rather than a lack of impact. 
Geopolitical shocks can affect energy prices rapidly, 
but such events are rare. Their effects are primarily 
transmitted through fuel markets, particularly gas 
prices, which directly influence marginal costs. As a 
result, geopolitical risk plays a more indirect role in 
extreme price events, operating through fuel cost 
adjustments rather than acting as a dominant short- 
term driver.
Overall, these findings are broadly consistent 
with existing literature on electricity price behav -
iour, which emphasises the roles of demand variabil -
ity, generation mix, and fuel costs in shaping price 
volatility (Lago et al., 2018 ; Nowotarski & Weron, 
2018 ). At the same time, the results provide add -
itional insights into how these factors operate differ -
ently in the context of extreme price events, 
particularly in terms of asymmetry and interaction 
effects.
From a decision-support perspective, these 
insights have direct practical implications.

3.5. Cross-market validation
To evaluate the generalisability of the proposed fore -
casting approach, we extend the analysis to add -
itional electricity markets, including France and 
Italy. These markets differ in generation mix, mar -
ket structure, and price dynamics, providing a suit -
able test for assessing the robustness of the model 
beyond a single-country setting.
All experimental settings, including feature con -
struction, model configuration, and hyperparameter 
optimisation procedures, are kept consistent with 
those used for the German market. This ensures 
that the cross-market evaluation reflects the intrinsic 
generalisability of the proposed approach rather 
than differences arising from model tuning.
Table 10 reports the out-of-sample (test set) per -
formance of the weighted-XGBoost model across 
Germany, France, and Italy, in terms of AUC and 
G-Mean for each time window (15-, 30-, and 45- 
day) as well as their averages. The results show that 
the model maintains consistently strong forecasting 
performance across markets and across different 
forecasting horizons. In particular, the AUC values 
remain high for both extremely high and low price 
events, indicating that the model effectively captures 
the underlying patterns associated with extreme 
price occurrences.
Some variation in performance is observed across 
markets, particularly for extremely low prices, where 
G-Mean values are relatively lower in France and 
Italy. This can be attributed to the increased com -
plexity and market-specific nature of low price 
events, which are often driven by renewable gener -
ation and system-specific conditions. Despite this, 
the model continues to deliver robust forecasting 
performance across all markets.
Overall, these findings provide evidence that the 
proposed approach generalises well to different elec -
tricity markets and remains stable across different 
forecasting horizons. This supports its potential 
applicability for broader use in electricity market 
analysis, operational planning, and risk 
management.
4. Conclusion
This study explored the application of weighted- 
XGBoost for forecasting extreme electricity prices in 
the context of imbalanced data, focusing on both 
extremely high and low price occurrences. The 
experimental results demonstrated the robustness 
and effectiveness of the proposed approach across 
different time windows in the German electricity 
market, with consistently strong performance across 
multiple evaluation metrics.
The G-Mean and ROC-AUC metrics confirmed 
the model’s ability to effectively distinguish between 
extreme and normal prices despite the inherent class 
imbalance. AUC values consistently exceeded 0.95 
across training and test datasets, highlighting the 
model’s high classification quality. Furthermore, 
compared to baseline models, weighted-XGBoost 
achieved superior performance, demonstrating 
higher forecasting accuracy and better robustness in 
handling class imbalance. This highlights its effect -
iveness in capturing the underlying patterns of 
extreme price occurrences.
The SHAP-based interpretability analysis provided 
further insights into the key drivers of extreme elec -
tricity prices. The results revealed distinct differences 
between the determinants of extremely high and 
extremely low prices. Extremely high prices arise 
from a complex interaction of supply-demand condi -
tions, historical price trends, and external market 
dynamics, making them more volatile and susceptible 
to external shocks. Forecasted residual load, grid 
load, and wind generation play crucial roles, along -
side fuel price factors such as gas, carbon, and coal 
prices, indicating a significant influence of fuel cost 
fluctuations. In contrast, extremely low prices are pri -
marily driven by forecasted residual load. The lack of 
strong contributions from price-related variables sug -
gests that low-price events are more structurally 
determined by demand-side imbalances and

While the proposed model demonstrates strong 
forecasting capabilities, there are areas for further 
improvement. First, this study defines extreme price 
events using the 90th and 10th percentiles for 
extremely high and low prices, respectively. Future 
research could explore stricter thresholds (e.g., 95th/ 
5th or 99th/1st percentiles) to assess their impact on 
forecasting accuracy. Second, while the volatility- 
based dynamic weighted threshold adapts to market 
fluctuations, alternative methods such as adaptive 
quantiles, distribution-based approaches, or extreme 
value theory (EVT) might provide additional per -
spectives. Third, while the overall classification per -
formance for forecasting extremely high prices is 
satisfactory, there is still room for improvement 
compared to extremely low price forecasts.
From the SHAP-based interpretability analysis, it 
is evident that certain external features, such as fuel 
prices, play a more significant role in forecasting 
extremely high prices. This suggests that incorporat -
ing additional external features, potentially those 
more directly relevant to extreme price events, could 
further enhance the model’s ability to accurately 
forecast extremely high prices. Future work could 
focus on identifying and integrating such external 
features to optimise the model’s forecasting power 
for these events. By addressing these areas, future 
studies can provide a more comprehensive under -
standing of extreme price dynamics and improve 
the robustness of forecasting models in increasingly 
complex electricity markets.
Overall, the proposed framework contributes to 
the Operations Research literature by integrating 
adaptive threshold design, imbalanced learning, and 
interpretable analysis to support data-driven deci -
sion-making in electricity markets. It provides a 
practical and interpretable tool for supporting oper -
ational planning, market monitoring, and risk man -
agement in increasingly complex electricity systems.
